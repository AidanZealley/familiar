Familiar installs as a background service on one computer. That computer keeps the conversation and its data. Your other devices reach it through a browser.

## Run the installer

<Tabs syncKey="platform">
<TabItem label="macOS">

```sh
curl -fsSL https://github.com/AidanZealley/familiar/releases/latest/download/install.sh | sh
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Linux">

```sh
curl -fsSL https://github.com/AidanZealley/familiar/releases/latest/download/install.sh | sh
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows">

```powershell
irm https://github.com/AidanZealley/familiar/releases/latest/download/install.ps1 | iex
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

It downloads Node 24 if you don't already have 24.19 or newer, installs the `familiar` command and starts guided setup.

## Finish setup

<Steps>

1. Name your assistant. Press Enter to keep the default, `Familiar`. You can [rename it later](/familiar/use/settings-and-maintenance/#commands).

2. Sign in with your ChatGPT subscription. Setup prints a sign-in link and opens it in your browser when it can. Over SSH or on a machine without a display, open the link on another device. When you finish, the browser lands on a page that won't load. Copy that page's full address and paste it into the terminal.

   This login is how Familiar reaches the model. It doesn't sign you in to Familiar.

3. Open the URL setup prints, `http://127.0.0.1:4317`, and say hello.

</Steps>

If you stop setup partway, run `familiar` again to pick up where it left off.

## Reach it from your other devices

If [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/download) is installed and signed in, setup publishes Familiar at a private HTTPS address on your tailnet and prints it. Open that address on your phone or laptop. HTTPS is also what makes [notifications](/familiar/use/reminders-and-notifications/) work.

Without Tailscale, Familiar listens only on `127.0.0.1`, so only the machine it runs on can reach it.

## Who can use it

An installed Familiar has one owner and no separate login. Anyone who can reach its address can read the conversation, change its sign-in and install tools. Tailscale access keeps it to your own devices. Don't expose it to the public internet.

## Platform support

macOS and Linux have been tested on real machines. Windows passes automated CI. It hasn't had real-machine testing yet.
